# Data availability and attribution

**Completed separate extension:** PVDAQ system 10 completed all eleven declared cases under `research/PRODUCING_EXTENSION_LOCK_V1.json`, after original-result exposure. It uses complete recorded-minute AC means, 1.12 kW DC normalization, inferred fixed MST and a full one-minute alignment sensitivity. Verification passed for 4,702,320 forecasts and 192,700 history slots; all eight analysis tables reproduced exactly. The [integrated manuscript](paper/build/manuscript.pdf) and [extension findings](research/PRODUCING_EXTENSION_FINDINGS.md) report no clear early benefit, a small late recovery penalty and failure of the 5% improvement gate. The nearby system adds no independent climate; source qualification does not certify plant behavior or meter timing.

**Source interpretation update:** The exact-zero sensitivity misses positive near-idle Colorado readings. A separate post-hoc audit finds 3,876/3,957 unique scored daylight targets below 1% of DC capacity, also retained under quality/alignment variants. A raw-minute spot check confirms the low signal before normalization; its physical cause remains unverified. See [the staff review and diagnostic provenance](research/staff_review_v2/STAFF_RESEARCH_AND_IEEE_REVIEW.md). No targets were excluded or substituted.

The measured-data experiments use the public **Photovoltaic Data Acquisition (PVDAQ) Public Datasets**, DOI [10.25984/1846021](https://doi.org/10.25984/1846021), distributed through the OEDI/PVDAQ public object archive. The [official dataset catalog](https://data.openei.org/submissions/4568) and [PVDAQ documentation](https://github.com/openEDI/documentation/blob/main/pvdaq.md) are the source authorities. The catalog declares Creative Commons Attribution 4.0; the acquired manifests retain the license statement and DOI. Data attribution is distinct from authorship of this research.

The original cohort is PVDAQ 4902 (NIST ground array, measured AC meter and reference-cell POA irradiance) and PVDAQ 4 (Colorado, measured AC and POA irradiance). Source years 2015 and 2016 informed development; the final protocol trains on 2016 and evaluates a reserved period in 2017. Site-specific instrument IDs, units, conversions, coordinate/capacity provenance, source keys, checksums and quality bounds are in the normalized contracts. The [NIST monitoring paper](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.122.040) by Matthew Boyd provides additional measurement and timestamp documentation.

Files are reproducible from public objects identified in `data/raw/pvdaq_<system>_<year>/manifest.json`. Those exact snapshots, rather than a silently updated live dataset, define this experiment. Normalized hourly tables preserve source completeness and missingness. The final qualification manifest records alternate quality bounds and Colorado timestamp alignment. Exclusion ledgers and the original unmodified snapshot hashes remain available locally.

Receipt timestamps used in the experiments are **simulated**. The study does not claim access to measured network arrival logs, actual communications incidents or plant-fault labels. It must not describe simulated interruptions as observed outages. Recorded AC zeros are not relabeled as communication failures; a separate scoring sensitivity exposes one defined ambiguity pattern.

The small example under `examples/` generates entirely synthetic measurements. Its output may be inspected without downloading measured data and must never be substituted for the measured-data results in the paper.

The vendored IEEEtran package has its own upstream LPPL license, recorded under `paper/vendor/`; that license does not license this repository's original research code. Public code-release terms and author consent have not yet been selected. No public repository, dataset mirror or artifact DOI has been created. A final availability statement should name only a real, approved release location; do not insert a fictitious anonymous URL or DOI.
