# Frozen final evaluation protocol, version 4

Prepared 2026-09-12, before any 2017 forecasting-performance evaluation. The machine-readable specification is `configs/final_evaluation_v4.json`. The lock file records the actual UTC freeze time, configuration, code, tests, data contracts, normalized inputs and reused 2015 model hashes. A changed frozen dependency must stop execution. This is a prospective internal freeze, not a public preregistration or proof against undisclosed analyses.

## Decision and evidence boundary

The paper tests whether recovery-age grouping adds value beyond the same calibration kernel with availability grouping. It also documents receipt-time replay and the limits of interpreting recovery improvements. The inspected 2015/2016 results are development evidence. They do not establish a recovery-specific advantage. Neither a positive final result nor meeting the original PRD's proposed 5% threshold is assumed.

The 2017 data were downloaded and screened only for source provenance, completeness, units, timestamp alignment and the predefined quality variants. No 2017 forecast performance was used to select models, methods, schedules, endpoints or sensitivity limits. This statement applies to this repository's recorded work; it is not a claim that these public data have never been studied elsewhere.

The main endpoint is **recorded hourly AC output**, clipped at zero after averaging signed minute measurements. It is not latent available solar production. Production shutdowns and measurement problems cannot be reliably separated using the acquired channels. Observed zero output is retained. A prespecified secondary analysis excludes scoring targets with AC exactly zero and observed target POA irradiance above 200 W/m², without labeling them erroneous or changing model fitting. Missing labels are never imputed for training or scoring. This is a documented narrowing of the original PRD's desired fault-cause separation.

## Cohort, input contracts and temporal separation

Two geographic sites are fixed: NIST PVDAQ system 4902 (270.7 kW DC) and Colorado system 4 (1 kW DC). Site selection followed documented development-data qualification. Maryland 1200 was excluded because no measured irradiance observations were acquired for its 2016 candidate year. These are two fixed sites, not a representative population sample.

NIST uses a dedicated measured AC meter and reference-cell POA irradiance. Its native fixed EST timestamps and provided UTC establish the primary alignment. Colorado uses measured AC converted from W to kW and POA irradiance, with UTC inferred from a fixed seven-hour offset. Colorado minute-start versus minute-end alignment is unresolved; the complete train/evaluation pipeline is repeated after subtracting one minute from raw timestamps. Normalization requires 60 distinct valid minute readings in each hourly bin for each stream independently. Original data and all exclusions remain available through source manifests and quality audits.

Primary quality bounds are AC from −0.05 to 1.5 times DC capacity, and POA from −20 to 2000 W/m². The sensitivity tightens these to −0.01 to 1.2 times capacity and −10 to 1500 W/m². Small negative POA readings within bounds remain observations. NIST has identical primary and tighter inputs; Colorado changes only a few irradiance bins. Input variants were qualified before the freeze. Incomplete overlap bins from the alignment shift remain missing; conflicting nonmissing cross-year values are rejected.

All final primary forecasters fit only observed 2016 targets. Training origins begin January 3, 2016 at 00:01 UTC, and training targets must end by January 1, 2017 at 00:00 UTC. January 1–2, 2017 separates fitting from final replay. Replay starts January 3 at 00:01 UTC with empty calibration pools and pending-forecast state. January supplies causal calibration warmup. No forecast is issued between February 1 at 00:00 and 04:00 UTC; observer deliveries still proceed. Evaluation origins begin at 04:01 UTC on February 1 and end at 23:01 UTC on December 31. A scored target must end no later than January 1, 2018 at 00:00 UTC. Warmup results are stored separately and excluded from final comparisons. This year-based allocation supersedes the PRD's tentative percentage split, providing complete seasonal development and a separate final year.

## Forecast target and information availability

Each origin forecasts mean AC over a future one-hour interval ending at the next one, two, three or four clock hours. With the one-minute release margin, lead times to the interval end are exactly 59, 119, 179 and 239 minutes; these are not 60/120/180/240-minute point forecasts. A five-minute-margin sensitivity uses 55/115/175/235 minutes. The first interval has already begun at issue time; the model has no measurements from that unfinished interval. Forecast records contain target start/end, exact origin and actual lead.

Inputs are 24 consecutive hourly slots plus the 48th slot for each stream, latest received values, missing fractions, ages, currentness, transport ages and known calendar/solar geometry. Target geometry uses midpoint solar elevation calculated from static site coordinates. No future measured irradiance or archived weather forecast is provided. Inputs scale PV by DC capacity and irradiance by 1000. Base quantile models do not receive recovery age. Recovery age is derived from the observer's transition from missing current data to current data; injected outage plans are not given to forecasting or calibration.

The simulator modifies receipt times or drops messages. Measurement timestamps and values remain unchanged. A restored live observation bypasses the old backlog. Immediate backfill releases old packets at restoration; gradual backfill is oldest-first, starting one spacing after restoration; permanent loss never releases old packets. Later interruptions can intercept queued receipts. Only delivered PV labels may update calibrators, using the stored originally issued interval and state. Repeated stale labels cannot create additional targets or scores. Conflicting revisions are rejected rather than resolved using a future version.

## Fixed models and comparators

Development selected the flexible quantile profile and compact imputation profile for both sites. Flexible HGB uses 250 iterations, 31 leaves, minimum 40 leaf observations and learning rate 0.05; compact uses 150 iterations and 15 leaves, with the same other settings. Seven separate quantile losses at 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90 and 0.95 are fitted per horizon. No early stopping or final-data tuning is used. Seed is 20260911. Training is restricted to targets with positive midpoint solar elevation; night predictions are zero and excluded from the main daylight endpoint. Quantiles are sorted and clipped nonnegative. Training prediction variability and crossing rates are recorded. A degenerate fitted median stops execution before final evaluation. Zero-heavy development fits and their failed diagnoses are retained, not silently replaced.

Training augmentation concatenates clean replay with deterministic joint 12-hour immediate-backfill, PV-only 24-hour permanent-loss and irradiance-first 24-hour gradual-backfill replays. Observed labels may be repeated across augmentations; they are not independent measurements. Three augmentation schedules use model seed plus 1, 2 and 3. The no-block ablation fits clean replay only. The no-input-age ablation sets every explicit `*_age` feature to zero in both fitting and evaluation, including calibration state; missingness patterns and currentness still convey some age information, so this is not complete removal of information about staleness.

Primary scenarios compare fifteen outputs:

1. Native-missing-value quantile HGB without recalibration (`raw`).
2. Uniform rolling signed calibration (`rolling`).
3. Recency-weighted signed calibration (`recency`).
4. Physical-context kernel plus recency (`physical`).
5. The same kernel plus an availability-state local/global blend (`physical_availability`).
6. The same kernel plus availability and recovery-age grouping (`physical_recovery`).
7. A 50-bit exact missingness-mask blend (`mask50`).
8. Receipt-gated bounded adaptive alpha (`aci_bounded`).
9–10. Single and multiple input imputation with the same fitted point model.
11. Multiple imputation followed by receipt-gated recency calibration (`mi_recency`).
12–13. Residual-dressed latest-value and geometry-adjusted persistence.
14. Residual-dressed previous-day same-target-hour prediction, falling back to latest received PV when that slot is missing.
15. Quantile HGB with carry-forward lag filling and constant availability indicators (`carry_forward`). Filling proceeds from older to newer visible slots within each origin; unresolved oldest slots default to zero. It does not impute evaluation labels.

The imputation adaptation uses 50 training-only nearest donor pairs and 10 origin-keyed random draws. Its within variance is fitted-training residual MSE; multiple-input variance adds the finite-draw between-prediction term. Donor sampling remains invariant to batch order and future rows. It is an explicit adaptation of Pashmchi et al.'s Setup 2, with HGB and joint-channel donor extensions, not a reproduction of every experiment or method in that paper. Persistence residual quantiles use three target-solar strata with cut points 0.2 and 0.6; the geometric ratio has denominator floor 0.1 and cap 3.

Calibration is horizon-specific, daylight-only, with a 720-hour issued-origin window, recency scale 336 hours, kernel bandwidth 1, minimum 30 scores and local/global shrinkage 50. The context vector contains known geometry, calendar, base median/width, visible irradiance/missingness, capped ages and missing fractions. The primary matched controls use exactly the same context, recency and shrinkage; availability grouping omits only the two recovery-age group coordinates. The bounded adaptive comparator uses step 0.001 and clips alpha to [0.01, 0.99]. Signed corrections may shrink intervals, followed by outward nesting around an unchanged median and nonnegative projection. These engineering choices invalidate any automatic appeal to the original exchangeable CQR or unrestricted ACI guarantees. Coverage is an empirical diagnostic.

The mask blend is not CP-MDA, distributional-imputation conformal prediction or the missingness-adaptive wind-forecasting algorithm. The original PRD explicitly allows a narrower claim if its B5/B6 literature reproductions cannot be implemented credibly. Here we study matched empirical calibration controls and an explicit PV-MI adaptation. We do not claim superiority over unimplemented published algorithms or novelty for missingness-aware prediction itself. Exact distinctions and primary references are in `CLOSEST_METHOD_CROSSWALK.md`.

## Scenarios and sensitivities

Eight primary cases are clean, PV-only loss, irradiance-only loss, joint immediate backfill, joint gradual backfill, joint permanent loss, irradiance-first and PV-first restoration. Each uses 12-hour interruption, three-hour channel restoration gap where applicable, 15-minute backlog spacing and schedule seed 20261012. Fault starts are sampled over all 24 UTC hours, once every seven days, independently of observed weather or performance. The `clean` case has no injected faults even though its scheduling-family field is `joint`; all analysis must use `case_id`.

Seventeen additional named cases vary one declared element from joint-gradual, except where their name specifies a different mechanism: 6/24-hour duration; 60-minute backfill spacing; six-hour irradiance-first channel gap; schedule seeds 20261013–20261016; tighter quality bounds; Colorado-only minute-end alignment; 2015-trained weights; five-minute normal receipt margin; no explicit input age; no block augmentation; privileged label delivery; cross-site quantile weights; and stale transport with permanent loss. Stale transport repeats the last pre-interruption observed value with its original measurement end and a new receipt/transport ID at normally scheduled message times. It cannot refresh measured state.

This is 25 cases and 49 site/case combinations. Five fixed schedules apply to the joint-gradual experiment; they are not a full five-seed cross-product of every scenario. The 1/3-hour interruptions suggested in the initial PRD are omitted to prioritize the explicitly declared 6/12/24-hour stress range. Sensitivities compare raw, recency, physical, availability and recovery outputs. The privileged-label diagnostic exposes normally delivered PV labels despite interrupted inputs; it is excluded from any deployable-method ranking. It intentionally demonstrates an information-access change, not a valid operational comparator.

The temporal sensitivity reuses archived 2015-trained models with fresh January 2017 calibration. Cross-site sensitivity transfers normalized quantile weights and uses the target site's known capacity/coordinates and causal local calibration. Both sites informed development; this is not pristine held-out-site methodology development, and locally calibrated results are not zero-adaptation transfer. The site-specific raw transferred output is reported alongside locally calibrated variants.

## Endpoints, aggregation and uncertainty

Eligible main rows have an observed valid target, positive target midpoint solar elevation, an origin in the evaluation period and a target ending within the period. Every compared method uses exactly these same rows. Primary error is weighted interval score (WIS) normalized by static DC capacity, using the median plus 50%, 80% and 90% central intervals. Report normalized MAE, all three empirical coverages and normalized 90% width alongside WIS. Raw-unit values are recoverable by capacity; site-level tables must retain units. Report phase, horizon, calendar and event support counts, calibration fallback frequency and effective pool support.

Primary scientific contrast is recovery minus availability NWIS, reported separately for each site and for recovery 0–6 and 6–24 hours after full restoration, with all four horizons and seven faulted primary scenarios given equal weight where supported. Also report the combined recovery endpoint, outage and partial-restoration effects, and clean behavior. Secondary contrasts are recovery minus physical and physical minus recency. A positive difference means worse forecasting. Main absolute method tables give equal weight to scenarios and horizons, rather than letting the largest phase dominate. Per-case/per-horizon tables remain available. Actual row counts accompany every aggregate, and unsupported strata remain missing rather than zero.

For uncertainty, aggregate paired same-origin/horizon differences by UTC calendar day, keeping common-weather scenarios and seeds together. Use a paired calendar-block bootstrap with 10,000 replicates, seed 20260912, and nonoverlapping 7-day blocks anchored at February 1, 2017; 14- and 28-day blocks are sensitivity checks. Resample blocks within a fixed site, recomputing the equal-scenario/horizon estimand from sums and counts. Include the final shorter block. Report percentile 95% intervals and number of available days/blocks/events. These conditional fixed-site intervals do not establish population generalization; the two sites are not bootstrap replicates. Overlapping horizons, scenarios and seed replays must not be treated as independent rows. Sensitivity intervals are exploratory and unadjusted; all main site/phase contrasts are disclosed together, with no selected-significance claim.

The PRD's proposed signal gate is at least 5% lower failure/recovery NWIS than the development-selected physical-context comparator. Clean degradation must be no greater than `max(0.02 × physical NWIS, 0.0001)` at each site's equal-horizon aggregate; the absolute NWIS floor is fixed here before evaluation. Report both point estimates and paired intervals rather than converting these engineering thresholds into a statistical guarantee. Nominal 90% coverage within five percentage points is a diagnostic target. The desired support is 30 distinct events and 200 daylight cases per reported recovery stratum; under-supported cells are flagged, not dropped. A null or adverse outcome remains an admissible paper finding.

## Reproducibility, amendments and submission gates

The final runner refuses overwritten output directories and verifies the lock before execution. It archives source, configuration and initial empty calibration state, then records fitted models, donor dates, source hashes, schedules, feature tables, per-forecast outputs, diagnostics and summaries. Post-run analysis scripts may be added after the lock but must implement the above endpoints and record their own hashes. Any necessary forecast-code repair requires a dated amendment, a fresh lock and a fresh run directory, with the reason and exposed performance scope explicitly recorded. Failed runs remain identifiable and cannot be silently presented as untouched tests.

The executable configuration uses JSON rather than the PRD's proposed YAML; the semantics and exact bytes are versioned. Original PRD bytes remain unchanged. Release work will supply a small explicitly synthetic example, environment specification, adapters, evidence tables, figures, model card, manuscript and commands for reproduction. An automated independent verifier does not satisfy the PRD's desired second human contributor reproduction. Human authorship, consent, reference/claim review and that reproduction remain explicit pre-submission gates.

The conference's official submission and CFP pages are the authority for the seven-page regular-paper target and anonymous review. A reviewable manuscript is not an externally submitted paper. No upload, payment, organizer contact or authorship commitment is authorized by this research goal. Substantive AI assistance is recorded truthfully; anonymity-compatible placement of the required disclosure remains a submission-format issue, not grounds for concealing assistance.
